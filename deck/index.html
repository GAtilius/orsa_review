1 · Problem statement

Does ORSA training prepare officers for the jobs they actually get?

DA PAM 600-3 expects every FA49 officer to be a subject matter expert in eight skill areas, whatever the assignment. The Army does not know whether initial training, through graduate school (ACS) or ORSA-MAC, builds the skills first assignments need, or whether the list still reflects how ORSAs and data scientists work today.

1

The FA49 section of the DA PAM 600-3 Smartbook lists eight areas in which every ORSA serves as a subject matter expert, with no difference by assignment. Officers qualify either through ORSA-MAC or an approved master’s degree. This study asks whether that training matches what first assignments need, and whether the list itself should change.

2 · Study issues

Four questions this study answers

1

How well did training and education prepare FA49 officers for their first ORSA assignment?

Data: overall agreement rating and officers’ explanations

2

Which listed skills did first assignments need, and where did training miss?

Data: nine skill ratings, recoded into trained vs. needed

3

Do results differ by training path (ACS, ORSA-MAC, both) and by type of first assignment?

Data: comparisons across training path, assignment, degree and degree requirement

4

What should be added to, or removed from, the FA49 skill list?

Data: free-text answers, working group feedback, DA PAM 600-3, civilian ORSA and data science practice

2

Issues 1 to 3 are answered directly from the survey. Issue 4 combines the survey’s free-text answers with our working group’s feedback and a comparison against how operations research and data science work in the civilian sector.

3 · Constraints, limitations and assumptions

What bounds these findings

Constraints

  • Survey questions were already fielded and could not be changed
  • Voluntary, self-selected respondents
  • The FA49 Smartbook section (June 2021) is the latest available
  • No access to job performance records

Limitations

  • 57 responses; some groups as small as 4
  • Self-reported, looking back on the first job
  • Skip logic hid the ACS skills question from all 11 NPS and AFIT graduates
  • Free-text themes coded once, not independently checked
  • Two responses took under 15 seconds; kept in

Assumptions

  • Respondents are broadly representative of FA49 officers
  • “Well trained” self-ratings reflect real preparation
  • First-assignment needs are a fair test of the skill list
  • Civilian ORSA and data science practice is a relevant benchmark

3

The biggest limitation is size: 57 responses, so comparisons between small groups such as combat analysts or technical-domain analysts are directional only. The NPS and AFIT skip-logic gap means the graduate-school skills question undercounts those two schools. None of this changes the overall pattern, but it is why the recommendations include a second, corrected survey wave.

4 · Methodology

Survey, measure, compare, then benchmark

Step 1

Collect

  • Survey of 57 FA49 officers, four waves, Nov 2024 to Oct 2025
  • Nine skill ratings, five answer choices each
  • Training path, degree, first job; three free-text questions

Step 2

Measure

  • Answer counts for every question
  • Skill answers recoded into trained and needed
  • Gaps: needed but not trained; trained but not used
  • Data-quality checks

Step 3

Compare

  • By training path: ACS, ORSA-MAC, both
  • By first assignment and degree
  • By the degree’s final requirement (thesis, capstone, coursework)

Step 4

Synthesize

  • Theme free-text answers
  • Benchmark against DA PAM 600-3 and civilian practice (BLS, INFORMS)
  • Add working group input
  • Build a tiered skill framework

Civilian sources: BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook (operations research analysts, data scientists); INFORMS analytics job task analysis

4

Each skill rating had five answers. “Trained” means the officer said they were well trained. “Needed” combines well trained and needed, learned it on the job, and not trained with a negative effect on performance. Comparing the two gives the gap measures used in the results. The working group’s seven requested skills were added at the synthesis step.

5 · Results and recommendations

Training mostly works; the skill list needs tiers

Results

  • 82% say training prepared them; only 3 of 57 disagree.
  • Over-taught: 22 officers trained in optimization and 19 in M&S never needed it.
  • Under-taught: 59% of those who needed assessments learned it on the job.
  • Needed by nearly all: data communication (95%) and data analysis (88%).
  • Paths differ: 80% of ORSA-MAC vs. 37% of ACS graduates felt ready for studies and assessments.
  • Jobs differ: instructors most satisfied (5.6 of 6), combat analysts least (4.6).

Recommendations

Foundation

All ORSAs, day one: problem framing, data collection and surveys, statistics, SQL and Army data platforms, programming, basic apps and dashboards, AI literacy, operations assessment, communication and teaching

Core

Working proficiency: cost-benefit analysis, optimization, applied M&S, predictive modeling, decision analysis

Specialty

One or two, by assignment: AI/ML and agentic AI, app development, data engineering and cloud, campaign assessment, large-scale M&S, PPBE

  • Remove “other big data specialties”; merge forecasting into predictive modeling; recast studies as problem framing.
  • Add an assessments bridge for ACS-only graduates; require a thesis or capstone.
  • Field a second survey wave on the new skills, with the skip logic fixed.

5

Results answer study issues 1 to 3; the recommendations answer issue 4. The three tiers keep the classic OR methods but stop expecting every officer to be an expert in all of them. The working group’s items appear in every tier: data platforms, surveys, apps, AI literacy and operations assessment in the foundation; cost-benefit analysis and M&S in the core; agentic AI, app development and campaign assessment as specialties. Survey and working group disagree on M&S: the survey shows it is often taught but unused, the group wants more. Keeping applied M&S in the core and deep M&S as a specialty covers both, and the second survey wave should test it.
